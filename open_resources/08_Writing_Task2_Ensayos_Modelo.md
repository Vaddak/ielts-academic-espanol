## 1. Opinion · consigna nº3
> *Cities should ban private cars from their historic centres. To what extent do you agree or disagree?*

In many cities, narrow medieval streets that were designed for pedestrians and carts are now crowded with traffic. **I largely agree that** private cars should be banned from historic centres, **although** some exceptions are necessary.

**The main argument for a ban is** the protection of the centre itself. Vibrations and exhaust fumes gradually damage old buildings, while traffic noise and parked cars make it difficult for residents and visitors to enjoy them. When Pontevedra, in Spain, removed most cars from its old town, the streets were quickly filled with cafés, markets and children playing, and local businesses reported more customers rather than fewer. **This suggests that** the economic fears often raised by shopkeepers are exaggerated.

**A ban would also** encourage people to use public transport, cycling and walking, which benefits public health and reduces emissions across the whole city. **Once** drivers know that they cannot enter the centre, they tend to change their habits within a few months, **particularly if** park-and-ride services are available on the outskirts.

**Nevertheless,** a complete ban would be unfair to certain groups. Elderly and disabled residents, emergency services and delivery vehicles all need access, and **it would be unrealistic to expect** a plumber to carry heavy equipment on a bus. **For this reason,** the most effective policies allow permits for these groups, or restrict deliveries to early morning hours.

**In conclusion, I largely agree that** private cars should be excluded from historic centres, **since** the benefits for heritage, health and local life clearly outweigh the inconvenience to drivers, **provided that** essential access is guaranteed for those who genuinely need it.

**🔎 Por qué es 7+:** postura con matiz (*largely*) que se mantiene **igual** en la conclusión · ejemplo concreto (Pontevedra) · la concesión tiene su propio párrafo y se resuelve · cada párrafo abre con una frase-tema clara.

## 2. Discussion · consigna nº4
> *Some people think that children should start learning a foreign language at primary school, while others believe it is better to wait until secondary school. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

There is ongoing debate about the best age to begin learning a foreign language. **While** some people argue that children should wait until secondary school, **I believe that** starting at primary school offers significant advantages.

**Those who favour a later start argue that** young children should first master their mother tongue. They point out that primary teachers are not always trained language specialists, and that one or two short lessons a week may achieve very little. **In their view,** older students, who can understand grammar explicitly and work more independently, make faster progress in less time, which allows schools to use their limited teaching hours more efficiently. **There is some truth in this:** a twelve-year-old can often learn in one year what a seven-year-old learns in three.

**However,** speed is not the only consideration. Younger children are generally less afraid of making mistakes, and they imitate sounds more accurately, which helps them develop better pronunciation. **Moreover,** an early start gives learners many more years of contact with the language by the time they finish school. **Perhaps most importantly,** positive early experiences, such as songs, games and stories, can create a lasting interest in other languages and cultures, **whereas** students who begin as teenagers may see the subject as just another exam.

**In my opinion,** the benefits of an early start outweigh the drawbacks, **as long as** lessons are frequent and taught by qualified teachers. **Rather than** delaying language learning, governments should invest in training primary teachers, **so that** children can take full advantage of the years when they learn most naturally.

**🔎 Por qué es 7+:** la vista contraria se presenta **con justicia** (*There is some truth in this*) antes de rebatirla · la opinión aparece en la intro **y** en la conclusión · conectores variados (*Moreover, Perhaps most importantly, whereas*).

## 3. Advantages and disadvantages · consigna nº7
> *More and more people are choosing to live alone. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?*

In many countries, single-person households are now the fastest-growing type of home. **Although** this trend brings certain risks, **I would argue that** its advantages are greater.

**The most obvious disadvantage is** loneliness. People who live alone, especially the elderly, may go days without a meaningful conversation, and research has linked social isolation to depression and even heart disease. **In addition,** living alone is expensive: one person must pay the full rent and bills, and **this** contributes to housing shortages, **since** more homes are needed for the same number of people. In cities where rents are already high, this pressure is felt most strongly by young people on low incomes.

**On the other hand,** living alone offers independence and freedom. Young adults who manage their own homes learn to budget, cook and take responsibility for their decisions, which are valuable skills for later life. **Furthermore,** having a private space allows people to rest and concentrate, **which** many find difficult in shared flats. **It is also worth noting that** living alone is not the same as being alone: many people who live by themselves have active social lives, and modern technology makes it easier than ever to stay in touch with family and friends.

**On balance,** I believe the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages. Loneliness is a serious problem, **but** it is better addressed through community centres and social activities **than by** discouraging people from living independently. **For most people,** the freedom and personal growth that come with living alone are worth the extra cost.

**🔎 Por qué es 7+:** el lado que **pierde** va primero y el que **gana** después · el veredicto es explícito (*On balance*) · la conclusión propone **cómo mitigar** la desventaja, no se limita a repetir.

## 4. Problems and solutions · consigna nº10
> *In many cities, young people cannot afford to buy or rent a home. What problems does this cause, and what measures could be taken to solve them?*

The rising cost of housing has made it increasingly difficult for young people in many cities to find an affordable home. **This essay will examine** the main problems this causes and suggest some possible solutions.

**The first problem is** that young adults are forced to delay key stages of life. Many continue living with their parents well into their thirties, postponing marriage, children and financial independence. **A second, wider problem is** the effect on cities themselves. When young workers cannot afford to live near their jobs, they either face long and tiring commutes or move to other cities entirely, **leaving** employers such as hospitals and schools struggling to recruit staff. Over time, this can make a city older, less diverse and less economically dynamic, as the young people who drive innovation settle elsewhere.

**To tackle the first problem,** governments could increase the supply of affordable rental housing. Vienna, for example, has built municipal flats for decades, and **as a result** around half of its residents live in subsidised homes with reasonable rents. Rent controls and tax incentives for developers who build smaller, cheaper flats could have a similar effect. **As for the second problem,** cities could provide housing specifically for key workers near hospitals and schools, **as well as** improving public transport, **so that** living further away becomes a realistic option rather than a burden.

**In conclusion,** unaffordable housing delays young people's independence and weakens essential services. **However,** with greater public investment in rental housing and targeted support for key workers, **these problems could be significantly reduced.**

**🔎 Por qué es 7+:** cada solución está **conectada** a su problema (*To tackle the first problem… As for the second problem*) · ejemplo real y verificable (Viena) · el lenguaje tentativo es apropiado (*could, would*).

## 5. Direct (two-part) question · consigna nº14
> *Fewer people today write letters or postcards by hand. Why has this happened? Is this a positive or negative development?*

Handwritten letters and postcards, once the main way of keeping in touch over long distances, have become increasingly rare. **This is largely due to** digital technology, and **in my view** the change is mostly positive, **although** something valuable has been lost.

**The main reason for the decline is** speed and convenience. An email or text message arrives within seconds and costs nothing, **whereas** a letter may take days to arrive and requires paper, a stamp and a trip to the post office. **Smartphones have also changed** how we share experiences: instead of sending a postcard from a holiday, people now post photographs online that all their friends can see immediately. **As a result,** for most everyday communication, handwriting simply offers no practical advantage, and many young people have rarely, if ever, written a personal letter by hand.

**On the whole, I consider this a positive development.** Instant communication allows families separated by migration to stay closely connected, and it has made business and education far more efficient. **That said,** the decline of handwritten letters has a cost. A letter requires time and effort, and **for this reason** it often expresses feelings more thoughtfully than a quick message. Letters are also physical objects that can be kept for generations, **while** digital messages are easily lost. For people with disabilities that make handwriting difficult, digital tools have also made written communication far more accessible.

**In conclusion,** people write fewer letters mainly because digital alternatives are faster and cheaper. **Overall, this is a positive change,** but handwritten messages still have a special value, **and it is worth keeping** them for important occasions.

**🔎 Por qué es 7+:** la intro **responde las dos preguntas** desde el principio · un body por pregunta · la respuesta a "positive or negative" es clara pero matizada (*That said…*).

## 🧰 Las frases que se repiten en los cinco (llévatelas)
| Función | Frases |
|---|---|
| Postura | *I largely agree that… · I would argue that… · In my view,…* |
| Frase-tema | *The main argument for X is… · The most obvious disadvantage is… · The first problem is…* |
| Añadir | *In addition, · Furthermore, · Moreover, · Perhaps most importantly,* |
| Contrastar | *However, · Nevertheless, · That said, · whereas · On the other hand,* |
| Conceder | *There is some truth in this. · It is also worth noting that…* |
| Condición | *provided that · as long as · particularly if* |
| Resultado | *As a result, · For this reason, · so that* |
| Veredicto | *On balance, · Overall, · In conclusion,* |

⚠️ Fíjate en lo que **no** aparece en ningún modelo: *Although,* con coma · *a research* · *the* con nombres generales · *presented* como "mostró".
